ERRATUM (2026-10-13): vector accounting and timing baselines in the two AES-GCM records
=====================================================================================

Applies to EVIDENCE-2026-10-10-aes-gcm-run1-FAILED.txt and EVIDENCE-2026-10-10-aes-gcm-run2-FAILED.txt.
The raw records are left exactly as produced; this note is the correction. Found in review of #651.

1. Vector accounting. The "vector case counts" section of both records says "executed = parsed unless noted" and
   notes only the ChaCha20-Poly1305 exclusion. It omits the AES-GCM exclusions, so both records imply that all 316
   Wycheproof AES-GCM cases were executed. Only 133 were: 103 cases use AES-192 and 80 use an IV that is not 96
   bits (the typed API supports AES-128/256 with a 96-bit IV and a 16-byte tag only). The 183 skipped cases are
   counted and pinned in crates/foundation/rusty_aead/tests/aes_gcm_vectors.rs (EXECUTED = 133, SKIPPED_AES192 =
   103, SKIPPED_IV_NOT_96_BITS = 80). collect.sh now prints the executed and skipped counts for this file, so
   future records state them.

2. Timing baselines were not workload-matched. A leak test is judged against the A/A baseline measured most
   recently for its crate (allowance 2 x baseline alarms + 1). In both records the AES tests were judged against
   one baseline, "A/A AES-128-GCM seal", which seals with a key constructed before timing starts. That does not
   match:
   - "aes-128-gcm seal: fixed vs other key" and "aes-256-gcm seal: fixed vs other key", which time the
     constructor as well as the seal;
   - "aes-128-gcm open: first-byte vs last-byte tag mismatch", an invalid-tag open.
   Matched controls now exist (aes128_ctor_null_calibration_identical_classes,
   aes256_ctor_null_calibration_identical_classes, aes_open_null_calibration_identical_classes) and collect.sh runs
   each immediately before the test it calibrates. The two records were not regenerated. In both records the AES
   rows had 0 alarms (run 1: 1 alarm of 40 on the plaintext test, within its allowance), so the verdicts do not
   depend on the baseline choice, but the allowances used were preliminary.

3. The same mismatch exists for ChaCha20-Poly1305 and predates this work: its "fixed vs other key" test includes
   the constructor and its baseline is a seal with a key set up front, and its tag-mismatch test is an invalid-tag
   open judged against that seal baseline. This was not changed here.

4. A coverage statement. The exhaustive 256-byte S-box-against-its-definition test was dropped when the ct64
   layout replaced the first one, while the design note and fragment kept citing it as current. It is restored in
   src/aes.rs (bitsliced_sbox_matches_the_definition_for_every_byte), with a SubWord test.

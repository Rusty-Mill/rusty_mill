## 2026-10-24 - rusty_h2 `closed()`; rusty_tonic drops a cancelled handler

- **Why:** found by the `rusty_a2a` stage B design check (a spike put a `tokio` engine behind a `rusty_tonic` server). A handler that was only waiting (on a lock, on work running elsewhere) never learned its client had gone: it saw `CANCELLED` only on its next read or write, and once a request had been read in full `rusty_h2` forgets a reset stream, so a handler that never wrote could run to the end of its work for nobody. `tonic` (through `hyper`) drops the handler on a reset. Prerequisite PR0 of that stage, approved with it.
- **Added (`rusty_h2`):** `driver::Closed`, a future made by `Respond::closed()` or `SendStream::closed()`. It does not borrow the `Respond`, so it can be raced against a handler that owns it. It resolves when the stream is reset by the peer, the connection fails, or the stream is finished and forgotten, and at once if it already is. Every waiter is woken (a per-stream list of wakers, filled by `Closed::poll`, drained when the stream fails or is forgotten).
- **Changed (`rusty_tonic` server):** `Router` races each handler against `Respond::closed()`. When the stream goes first the handler is dropped and nothing is sent (there is no one to send to); a deadline still ends the call `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`. Behaviour change: a handler is no longer given `CANCELLED` from its next `send` or `read` after a cancel; it is dropped before that. The one test that asserted the old behaviour now asserts the drop.
- **Tests (written first; they failed to compile or failed before the change):** `rusty_h2/tests/driver.rs` (+8): pending while the stream lives; completes after a reset when the request was fully read, when it was still being sent, after the response started, when the connection dies, when the stream finishes normally; every one of two waiters woken; ready at once for a stream already gone. They assert promptness: a `timeout` polls its future once more at the deadline, which hid a missing wake-up as a pass after the full wait until the helper checked elapsed time. `rusty_tonic/tests/server_lifecycle.rs` (+3, one rewritten): an idle unary handler is dropped on cancel; a streaming handler waiting between replies; one actively sending (the rewritten test). A fourth, "a cancelled call gives its stream slot to the next" (`max_concurrent_streams(1)`), passes without the change, because `rusty_h2` already stops counting a reset stream; it is kept as a guard.
- **Verified:** 5 mutations of the new `rusty_h2` code (no wake in `fail_stream`, none in `forget_stream`, `abort` ignored, a forgotten stream treated as live, wakers never registered) and 2 of the router (the `closed` branch never firing, a `closed` taken from nothing): all killed after the promptness fix. The `rusty_h2` and `rusty_tonic` suites pass; 300 runs of `server_lifecycle` and 120 of `driver` at 60- and 40-way parallelism are clean (an earlier flake, a test that cancelled before its handler had started, is fixed).
- **Known limitations:** the signal is for streams the server accepted; a request reset before it is accepted is still reclaimed silently, as before.
- **Fixed (review follow-up):** connection termination resolves `Closed` even for a completed stream retained for unread response headers, DATA or trailers. All registered waiters are woken without discarding those buffered reads. Two deterministic driver regressions cover waiters registered before and after driver death; the first checks both wakers directly, and both verify the response still drains successfully.

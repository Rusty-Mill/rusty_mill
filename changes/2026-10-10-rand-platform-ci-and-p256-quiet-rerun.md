## 2026-10-10 - `rusty_rand` per-OS CI and the ECDH P-256 quiet-machine rerun

- **Added (CI):** job `rusty-rand-platforms`, selected by the planner flag `rand_platforms` when `rusty_rand` is affected or its workflow/planner paths change (including CI-only changes), and listed in `required-gate`. It runs `cargo test -p rusty_rand` on `macos-latest` (the other-Unix `/dev/urandom` path, which no job ran before) and `cargo check --all-targets` for `aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu` (the `getrandom(2)` plus `rusty_libc` arm on its second architecture) and `riscv64gc-unknown-linux-gnu` (the other-Linux `/dev/random` arm). Windows was already covered by the `windows-latest` workspace test shards. Planner flag and workflow wiring have tests (`test_ci_workflow.py`, including mixed CI/docs and pure CI regression cases, command coverage, and required-gate integration).
- **Added (evidence):** `docs/research/crypto-evidence/EVIDENCE-2026-10-10-ecdh-p256-quiet-rerun.txt`: the ECDH P-256 sparse-vs-dense test and its A/A baseline, two 40-repetition series on an idle machine, both reported, thresholds and allowance rule unchanged. 0 of 40 alarms in each series; leak-test maxima 4.43 and 3.12, A/A maxima 3.05 and 3.50.
- **Known limitations:** the cross targets are compile checks only; nothing runs the aarch64 or riscv64 backends, and the macOS job's first result is the first evidence the macOS path works. Both new CI paths were checked locally only for the two Linux cross targets (which compile); the macOS job and the workflow itself have not run yet. The rerun is a targeted two-test record, not a full `collect.sh` record; it does not explain the earlier maxima of 4.31 and 4.83, does not retire the watch item (run 1's 4.43 is close to the threshold) and does not prove constant time. Entropy quality and `/dev/random` blocking on kernels before 5.6 remain unverified.

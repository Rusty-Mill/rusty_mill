## 2026-11-05 - bbp: cgroup holder for mux-hosted agents

- **Added:** containment by cgroup for agents hosted in mux blocks (Let Em Loose D104, D108, card #732).
  - `bbp cgroup-open --cgroup PATH` records the intent under the task, claims the path for it (so a crash at any point leaves a record that leads quiesce back to the claim), creates the cgroup, and only then marks the record `created`. Each step is exclusive and runs under a per-task cgroup lock, and an ended task is refused. A path that nests in or around another claim is refused under a lock all tasks share. A claim is written in full first, then renamed into place, so a crash mid-write blocks no other open, and a claim another task drops during the scan is skipped. Also refused: a name with control characters or leading or trailing whitespace. Claims are kept per control directory but cgroups are global, so a session path must sit below the directory's scope, `lel-store-<16 hex digits of its canonical path's digest>` (`bbp cgroup-scope --dir D` prints it), and contain no other scope name: sessions of two directories can't nest. The `created` mark carries the cgroup's inode.
  - `bbp slot-hold --cgroup PATH [--deadline-ms N]` opens the cgroup once (start it after `cgroup-open`) and watches that cgroup, not the path, so one made again at the path is never killed. It keeps the slot until stdin closes and the cgroup is known to be empty. From the deadline on it writes `cgroup.kill`. A failed read or kill is reported and retried, and never frees the slot. It never signals a process group.
  - `bbp quiesce` also kills populated recorded cgroups and removes empty ones, including their empty child cgroups, with their claims and records. It does this under the same lock, and only for `created` records its task owns, and only while the cgroup at the path still has the recorded inode, read and killed through a handle opened and checked first. A cgroup made again at the path after a crash is left alone. An intent left by a crash is dropped without touching the cgroup.
- **Known limitations:** nothing uses these yet; the builder launch (slice 6) takes the slot through the holder and hands its fd to orch. The end-to-end tests skip without a writable cgroup2 directory.

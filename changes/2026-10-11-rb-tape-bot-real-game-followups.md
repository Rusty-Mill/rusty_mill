## 2026-10-11 - rb_tape_bot real-game follow-ups: overtime goal, named socket errors

- **Fixed:** `rb_match_log --overtime-goal` never shot. It required `game_time_remaining > 3.0` in overtime, but core reports 0.0 at the first overtime kickoff and then negative values (-39.0 at a goal 39 s in; RLBot rc17, found in the stage 4 real-game check). The decision is now `tie_up::overtime_goal_due(overtime, playing, sent)`, which does not look at the clock. The README's finite-match commands use `--seconds 480` and `840`, the windows that reached the tie-up and overtime in that check.
- **Changed:** `rb_rlbot_client` io errors name the socket call that failed (`read (poll)`, `set_nonblocking(false)`, `read (bounded)`, `write`); the original error stays reachable as the wrapped error's `source()`, though `raw_os_error()` on the outer error is `None`. A timeout or interruption is passed through unchanged. A Windows run had died with a bare `Overlapped I/O operation is in progress (os error 997)`; the cause is not established, and this only makes the next occurrence say where it came from.
- **Changed:** bounded reads (`recv_timeout`) are a nonblocking read with 1 ms naps until the deadline instead of a blocking read under `SO_RCVTIMEO`; only `recv` blocks, and no receive timeout is ever set. On Windows a bounded read had three times ended a long match with `Overlapped I/O operation is in progress (os error 997)`, pinned by the named errors to `read (timeout)`. Microsoft documents a connection's state after a timed-out blocking receive as indeterminate, and this client timed one out every few milliseconds by design. The cause of the 997 itself is not established.
- **Changed:** a send first puts the socket back in blocking mode (reads leave it nonblocking), so a write under backpressure waits for the peer instead of failing with `WouldBlock` after part of a frame was out. Test: a 24 MB `send_all` after a poll, with a reader that starts 300 ms late.
- **Verified on the game:** on the final transport (`7317d569`, RLBot rc17, Windows 11) the pogo, hivemind and two-team tape runs scored exactly as before (0 recorded inputs differing; position error mean 2.4, 1.7 and 6.0 uu), the probe advanced 120.0 frames/s, and two `--length five --seconds 840 --tie-up --overtime-goal` runs (about 14 minutes of window each) printed `overtime goal sent` and scored in overtime with no connection error. The overtime goal had also passed twice on the earlier `dfc54aec`.
- **Known limitations:** two clean long runs do not show the os error 997 drop is gone: before this transport it ended roughly one long match in two, so two clean runs would happen about one time in four by chance. The cause of the 997 is not established; if it recurs the error names the call. A bounded wait requests naps of at most 1 ms but timer granularity and scheduling can oversleep, so there is no latency bound (the tape results above are the only measurement of its effect). Not investigated: the pogo ball error in `rb-verify` (mean 203 uu in the first session) and the boost error (mean 12 to 21).

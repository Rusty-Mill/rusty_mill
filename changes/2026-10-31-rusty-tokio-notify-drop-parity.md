## 2026-10-31 - rusty_tokio: Notify drop parity with tokio

- **Fixed:** two cases where `Notify` differed from tokio 1.53 when a `Notified` or `OwnedNotified` was dropped after it was notified (#1303). First, a permit that `enable` took was banked again when the waiter was dropped, so the next `notified()` returned at once. In tokio the permit stays taken. Now `enable` returning `true` consumes the wakeup, as a `Ready` poll does, and dropping the waiter passes nothing on. This also covers a `notify_one` wakeup that `enable` reported. A future that returned `Ready`, or whose `enable` returned `true`, now stays `Ready` when a later poll finds the task's coop budget spent, as tokio's does. Before, that poll returned `Pending` and marked the wakeup unconsumed, so dropping the future passed it on a second time. A future that took a permit and returned `Ready` also no longer takes a second banked permit when it is polled again. Second, a `notify_last` wakeup that reached a waiter dropped before it resolved went to the oldest waiter. Now it goes to the next-newest one, as in tokio. Each waiter records whether `notify_one` or `notify_last` took it out of the queue, and a drop forwards the wakeup to the same end. This corrects the 2026-10-30 entry, which said a permit taken by `enable` is banked again "as tokio does". Eleven new tests cover this. Six are parity tests in `tests/sync.rs` that run the same calls on tokio's `Notify` and on ours, two of them cross-thread tests where a notify races the drop of the waiter it targets. Five are unit tests in `notify.rs` that force the window between taking a waiter and waking it, or poll with a spent coop budget. Ten of the eleven fail on the base. The eleventh, a `notify_one` racing a drop, passes there because that path already matched tokio. The existing unit test that expected the old re-banking now expects tokio's result.
- **Known limitations:** `Notify` still banks one permit for each `notify_one` made while nobody waits. tokio banks at most one.

## 2026-10-21 - rusty_h2: the async driver

- **Why:** phase 4a-2 of the first-party `prost`/`tonic` plan (card t45), on top of the sans-IO core from 4a-1. gRPC needs trailers and streaming over a real connection; `rusty_h2` had no I/O.
- **Added:** `rusty_h2::driver` (feature `rusty-tokio`, off by default so the default build stays dependency-free): `client()` and `server()` return handles plus a `ConnectionDriver` future to spawn. `ClientHandle::{send_request, ping}`, `Acceptor::{accept, graceful_shutdown}`, `SendStream` (waits for flow-control capacity), `RecvStream` (returns capacity as pieces are read; not reading is the backpressure), `Respond`, `Response`, `Body::{Data, Trailers}`. Dropping the last client handle or the acceptor winds the connection down after in-flight calls finish.
- **Behaviour:** a client waits for the server's first SETTINGS and for a free stream slot before it starts a request. Output the peer is not reading stops the driver reading more (a PING flood stalls the sender instead of growing memory). A dropped `SendStream` resets its stream (CANCEL); a dropped `Respond` resets it (INTERNAL_ERROR).
- **Changed (core):** GOAWAY no longer closes the sans-IO connection; the peer's already-accepted streams finish and only new streams are refused (RFC 9113 §6.8). Added `Connection::{goaway_received, send_capacity, settings_frame}`.
- **Tests:** 18 in `tests/driver.rs` (our client against our server, plus raw-frame hostile peers) and 14 in `tests/interop.rs` (live TCP against the `h2` crate as client and as server: trailers, 2 MiB bodies, 48 concurrent calls, 200 requests with up to 3 KB header values to cross-check both HPACK tables, resets with reasons, trailers-only responses, graceful shutdown, PING). `h2`, `http`, `bytes` and `tokio` are dev-dependency oracles only. Written first (30 failed on `todo!()`), then mutation-checked.
- **Found, not fixed:** `rusty_tokio`'s `DuplexStream` can deadlock on drop (it wakes a waker while holding the pipe lock, and the wake can drop the task owning the other half); about 1 in 150 runs of a 64-call test over `duplex`. The tests use loopback TCP instead. No connection-level WINDOW_UPDATE is sent at start, so one connection moves at most 64 KiB per round trip until reads release capacity. Push promises are refused, not delivered. No keepalive timer, no connection-level timeout, no TLS (those belong to `rusty_hyper`).
